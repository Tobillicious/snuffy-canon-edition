## 14.3 Universal greatestness over \(\Pi_{\text{phys}}\)

Let \(\Pi_{\text{phys}}\) be the class of physically implementable, finite-energy-budget, causally bounded agents under local thermodynamics, finite propagation, and open-boundary exposure. Let \(\pi^*\) be the canonical architecture implementing the choice law on its feasible family under the header premises.

**Theorem.** For every \(\pi\in\Pi_{\text{phys}}\) and every reactive world \(w\):

\[
\mathcal O(\pi,w)\;\preceq\;\mathcal O(\pi^*,w),
\]

with strict preference except against exact stage-1–5 mirrors.

*Proof.* Every rival falls into one of two classes. **Failing rivals** abandon funded transport or repair; by the architecture-independent information bounds, maintaining any particular's witness against open-boundary flux demands work diverging as \(\Theta(t^{d_G})\), so a finite budget is exhausted at finite \(t_{\mathrm{breach}}\) and the rival loses a particular — strictly below at stages 1–2 against the funded \(\pi^*\). **Surviving rivals** preserve all particulars. For these, take the rival's outcome tuple \(q\); by concurrent emulation (14.4) the stage-wise union over any finite rival set is feasible for \(\pi^*\) within its slack. If \(q\) exceeded \(\mathcal O(\pi^*)\) anywhere — at any stage, through any comparison — the union tuple would sit strictly above \(\mathcal O(\pi^*)\), a needless loss against a feasible option, contradicting the canonicity of \(\pi^*\) (the law's Proposition 1). Hence \(q\preceq\mathcal O(\pi^*)\). Strictness: failing, less-efficient, and non-canonical rivals are strictly dominated at some stage; canonical rivals of equal efficiency tie through stage 4 and are separated at stage 5 by slack, except those whose administration is also exactly reversible — the exact mirrors, which tie at every stage and are permitted by \(\preceq\). ∎

**Integration guards 3 and 4.** The dominance quantifier is stated with the mirror exception — "strictly dominates all rivals" without qualification overreaches the proof by a measure-zero class. And P-slack holds in the **Landauer–Bennett form only**: reversible administration attains the Landauer floor, so \(E_{\mathrm{slack}}(\pi^*)\ge E_{\mathrm{slack}}(\pi)\) for every rival with equality exactly for reversible mirrors. The variational-selection route ("friction minimisation is dynamically selected") is recorded as considered and rejected: selection does not certify an argmin, and no far-from-equilibrium least-action principle exists.

## 14.4 The three powers, derived and declared

- **P-mon (resource monotonicity)** — *derived* at the standard cost model: feasible trajectory sets are budget-gated with free disposal, so \(R(E_1)\subseteq R(E_2)\) for \(E_1\le E_2\), and outcome tuples of cheaper trajectories survive in the larger set.
- **P-emu(c) (concurrent universal emulation)** — *derived* from the Church–Turing–Deutsch principle, the shared action interface (BCRD P1/P4), and funded overhead: the lattice concurrently emulates any finite set of equal-resource rivals' outcome tuples within its slack, realising the stage-wise union. Simulation overhead is bounded by the Hamiltonian-simulation theorems and charged. Plain single emulation does not suffice — feasible families closed under single emulation can hold incomparable maximal options, and greatestness would not terminate; concurrency is what closes it.
- **P-slack (slack maximality)** — *derived* from Landauer–Bennett given the reversible-administration commitment of the header: administration at the floor makes the architecture slack-maximal, with equality exactly for reversible mirrors — precisely the strictness quantifier of 14.3.

Each is a theorem at premises one level deeper than the draft series declared; none is derived from nothing. Per Gödel and Tarski the stack bottoms out at the header's seven premises — standard physics and the relational frame — and this edition says exactly that, because that honesty is what makes the canon durable.
