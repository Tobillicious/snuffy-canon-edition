### Pillar 1: The Bulk-to-Boundary Holographic Map ($\mathcal{E}$)

#### **Lemma 1.1 (Isometric Embedding of Source Charge)**

Let $\mathcal{H}_{\text{bulk}}$ denote the interior state space equipped with source charge operator $Q_{\text{src}}$, and let $\mathcal{H}_{\partial}$ denote the boundary state space. Let $\mathcal{E}: \mathcal{H}_{\text{bulk}} \to \mathcal{H}_{\partial}$ be the holographic encoding map.

**Theorem:** If the bulk dynamics satisfy local Gauss-law constraints across every codimension-1 slice terminating at $\partial$, then $\mathcal{E}$ is an isometric embedding on the physical code subspace $\mathcal{C}_{\text{phys}} \subset \mathcal{H}_{\text{bulk}}$, such that:


$$\mathcal{E}^\dagger \mathcal{E} = \mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{C}_{\text{phys}}}$$


and the boundary image preserves the source charge spectrum:


$$\mathcal{E} \, Q_{\text{src}} \, \mathcal{E}^\dagger = Q_{\partial}$$

#### **Proof Sketch:**

1. **Flux-Charge Locking:** By the bulk field equations, any localized source charge $Q_{\text{src}} = \int_{\Sigma} \star J$ generates an asymptotic flux through the bounding surface $\partial\Sigma$.
2. **Boundary Condition Mapping:** Define $\mathcal{E}$ via the limit of bulk fields approaching the boundary $\partial$:

$$\mathcal{E}\vert{}\psi\rangle = \lim_{r \to \partial} \phi(r, x) \vert{}\psi\rangle$$


3. **Inner Product Preservation:** Because the symplectic form on the bulk phase space degenerates at the boundary only along pure gauge directions quotiented out in $\mathcal{C}_{\text{phys}}$, the boundary symplectic flux matches the bulk integral. Thus, $\langle \psi_1 \vert{} \psi_2 \rangle_{\text{bulk}} = \langle \mathcal{E}\psi_1 \vert{} \mathcal{E}\psi_2 \rangle_{\partial}$, proving isometry. $\blacksquare$

### Pillar 2: Historical Trace Preservation ($\mathcal{H}_{\text{hist}}$)

#### **Lemma 2.1 (Chronological Path Integral Embedding)**

Let a historical trajectory be defined as a parameterized curve in state space $\gamma: [0, \tau] \to \mathcal{S}$. Standard instantaneous holographic mappings discard internal temporal ordering. To prevent this loss, we define the history-augmented boundary algebra $\mathcal{A}_{\partial}^{\text{hist}}$.

**Theorem:** There exists a temporal smearing operator $\mathbb{K}$ mapping bulk historical trajectories into non-local boundary time-correlators such that for any historical query operator $H_{\text{query}}$, its expectation value is recoverable from boundary observables without degradation:


$$\langle \psi(t) \vert{} H_{\text{query}} \vert{} \psi(t) \rangle = \int_{0}^{\tau} dt' \, K(t, t') \langle \mathcal{E}\psi \vert{} \mathcal{O}_{\partial}(t') \vert{} \mathcal{E}\psi \rangle$$

#### **Proof Sketch:**

1. **Modular Flow Extension:** Construct the modular Hamiltonian $K_{\partial}$ for the boundary sub-region. By generalized Hamilton-Jacobi relations in relational dynamics, bulk interior operators at proper time $t'$ can be pulled back to the boundary using the bulk-to-boundary propagator adapted to the background metric induced by the source charge.
2. **Non-Degeneracy of Temporal Traces:** Provided the capacity of the boundary channel exceeds the Kolmogorov complexity of the historical trajectory $\gamma$, the mapping $\mathcal{H}_{\text{hist}} \to \tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\partial}$ has a trivial kernel. Therefore, distinct past histories do not collapse onto identical boundary states. $\blacksquare$

### Pillar 3: Reconstruction & Invertibility Conditions

#### **Lemma 3.1 (Work-Bounded Decoder Inversion)**

Let $\mathcal{D}: \mathcal{H}_{\partial} \to \mathcal{H}_{\text{bulk}}$ be the derived decoder constrained by the work algebra $\mathcal{W}$, such that the total energy expended in decoding is bounded by $E_{\text{max}}$.

**Theorem:** The decoder $\mathcal{D}$ reconstructs the bulk state $\vert{}\psi_{\text{bulk}}\rangle$ from boundary data $\rho_{\partial}$ with fidelity $F \geq 1 - \epsilon$ if and only if the capacity $C(\mathcal{W})$ satisfies:


$$C(\mathcal{W}) \geq S_{\text{rel}}(\rho_{\text{bulk}} \vert{}\vert{} \rho_{\text{reference}}) + \frac{1}{\beta} \langle \mathcal{H}_{\text{work}} \rangle$$


where $S_{\text{rel}}$ is the relative entropy of the bulk state relative to the vacuum, and $\beta$ is the inverse temperature parameter of the work algebra.

#### **Proof Sketch:**

1. **Knill-Laflamme Quantum Error Correction Analogy:** Because $\mathcal{E}$ is an isometry on $\mathcal{C}_{\text{phys}}$, errors or truncations introduced by finite boundary resolution act as quantum channels $\Lambda_{\partial}$.
2. **Energy-Cost Bound:** Applying Landauer-type limits within the work algebra $\mathcal{W}$, any reconstruction operator $\mathcal{D}$ that extracts historical and charge data must dissipate heat proportional to the mutual information between the discarded boundary modes and the recovered bulk degrees of freedom.
3. **Invertibility Limit:** When $C(\mathcal{W})$ surpasses the bound set by Lemma 3.1, the reconstruction error $\epsilon \to 0$, establishing exact invertibility for the derived decoder. $\blacksquare$
