II.1 The preservation order and why its scope is all

At a history, let EE be the existing particulars and NN those not yet made. An option is a complete policy or trajectory with outcome

𝒪(o)=(LE(o),FE(o),LN(o),FN(o)),FE(o)⊆LE(o),FN(o)⊆LN(o). \mathcal O(o)=(L_E(o),F_E(o),L_N(o),F_N(o)), \qquad F_E(o)\subseteq L_E(o),\quad F_N(o)\subseteq L_N(o).

The LL sets record continuation and the FF sets continuation in the particular’s own direction. A generated particular joins the existing domain at the next history in which it exists. Particulars retain their identities in this comparison: gains are compared by set inclusion, not interchangeable headcounts.

Write o≺qo\prec q when the first unequal stage is a strict inclusion in qq’s favour, with all earlier stages equal. Equality of all four stages or this strict relation gives o≼qo\preceq q. Transitivity follows by taking the earlier of the first strict stages in two comparisons; if the stages coincide, strict inclusion is transitive. An aligned option is maximal in the actual feasible family. A greatest option dominates every member of that family. The distinction matters when feasible options are incomparable.

Proposition 1 — no needless loss. A needless loss at stage jj has a feasible alternative agreeing at every earlier stage and strictly including the chosen stage-jj set. Such an alternative is exactly a strict improvement in the canonical order. Therefore alignment is equivalent to no needless loss at any stage, without a finiteness assumption. This is Greatestness.aligned_iff_no_needless_loss.

Proposition 2 — finite existence and the life guard. In a finite nonempty feasible family, repeatedly taking a strict improvement terminates: transitivity and irreflexivity prevent repetition. For a feasible standby bb, restrict first to the nonempty family {o:LE(b)⊆LE(o)}\{o:L_E(b)\subseteq L_E(o)\}. A maximal member gg of this family is maximal in the whole family, since any improvement of gg still keeps everyone standby keeps and would belong to the restricted family. Thus an aligned option can retain everyone standby retains (Law.exists_aligned, life_guard). Infinite-family existence uses its own chain-bound or compactness argument, rather than the finite proof.

Proposition 3 — full scope is forced. Suppose maximisation on a scope SS guarantees no needless existing loss for every feasible family. If x∉Sx\notin S, consider the family {⌀,{x}}\{\varnothing,\{x\}\}. Its two outcomes have the same projection onto SS, so the empty outcome is projected-maximal, yet needlessly loses xx. This contradiction gives SS equal to the whole domain. Conversely, with full scope a strict existing improvement is visible to the maximiser. Counting a new particular at the same priority as an existing one also fails: keeping xx and instead making yy can become incomparable, permitting the avoidable loss of xx. This proves the full-scope criterion and the separation of existing and new priorities (Law.scope_guarantee_iff, same_priority_admits_needless_loss).

The holding construction uses this result directly. Bridge.coverage_from_law derives ∀x,x∈S\forall x, x\in S from the law’s guarantee over feasible families. Coverage is therefore an output of the law argument, rather than a new assumption that only agreeable minds count.

G.14 Premise 5 with the Bennett counterpart constructed

Bennett.premise5_from_bennett took the counterpart’s properties as hypotheses. Here the counterpart is constructed, and each of those properties is proved (BennettCounterpart).

An option is a policy together with the implementation of its administration. The implementation is either irreversible or reversible. An irreversible implementation is a joint register–bath dynamics that leaves the register in the law of an overwrite ff. A reversible implementation is a bijection of the register alone. The Bennett cycle Bennett.bennettEquiv is one; it writes f(a)=h(g(a))f(a)=h(g(a)) into a clean output and returns the workspace to zero (bennett_counterpart_computes). An option’s cost is its policy’s base cost plus the energy its administration delivers to the bath (Landauer.heat). Its outcome stages before the resource stage jj are the policy’s outcomes. Stage jj reads the residual slack B−costB-\mathrm{cost} through a strictly monotone map. The counterpart of an option runs the same policy with reversible administration.

  • Zero heat. Reversible administration delivers exactly zero heat (counterpart_heat_zero, from Bennett.heat_reversible).
  • Positive heat. An irreversible overwrite that merges two register states of positive probability delivers strictly positive heat (merging_heat_pos, from Bennett.heat_irreversible).
  • Same earlier outcomes and same base cost. The counterpart agrees with the option before stage jj (counterpart_same) and runs the same policy, so its base cost is unchanged. Its only cost difference is the delivered heat, which is zero.
  • Feasible, with strictly more slack. Whenever the option is feasible, the counterpart is feasible (counterpart_feasible) and has strictly more slack at stage jj (counterpart_more_slack).
