## II.1 The preservation order and why its scope is all

At a history, let \(E\) be the existing particulars and \(N\) those not yet made. An option is a complete policy or trajectory with outcome

\[
\mathcal O(o)=(L_E(o),F_E(o),L_N(o),F_N(o)),
\qquad F_E(o)\subseteq L_E(o),\quad F_N(o)\subseteq L_N(o).
\]

The \(L\) sets record continuation and the \(F\) sets continuation in the particular's own direction. A generated particular joins the existing domain at the next history in which it exists. Particulars retain their identities in this comparison: gains are compared by set inclusion, not interchangeable headcounts.

Write \(o\prec q\) when the first unequal stage is a strict inclusion in \(q\)'s favour, with all earlier stages equal. Equality of all four stages or this strict relation gives \(o\preceq q\). Transitivity follows by taking the earlier of the first strict stages in two comparisons; if the stages coincide, strict inclusion is transitive. An aligned option is maximal in the actual feasible family. A greatest option dominates every member of that family. The distinction matters when feasible options are incomparable.

**Proposition 1 — no needless loss.** A needless loss at stage \(j\) has a feasible alternative agreeing at every earlier stage and strictly including the chosen stage-\(j\) set. Such an alternative is exactly a strict improvement in the canonical order. Therefore alignment is equivalent to no needless loss at any stage, without a finiteness assumption. This is `Greatestness.aligned_iff_no_needless_loss`.

**Proposition 2 — finite existence and the life guard.** In a finite nonempty feasible family, repeatedly taking a strict improvement terminates: transitivity and irreflexivity prevent repetition. For a feasible standby \(b\), restrict first to the nonempty family \(\{o:L_E(b)\subseteq L_E(o)\}\). A maximal member \(g\) of this family is maximal in the whole family, since any improvement of \(g\) still keeps everyone standby keeps and would belong to the restricted family. Thus an aligned option can retain everyone standby retains (`Law.exists_aligned`, `life_guard`). Infinite-family existence uses its own chain-bound or compactness argument, rather than the finite proof.

**Proposition 3 — full scope is forced.** Suppose maximisation on a scope \(S\) guarantees no needless existing loss for every feasible family. If \(x\notin S\), consider the family \(\{\varnothing,\{x\}\}\). Its two outcomes have the same projection onto \(S\), so the empty outcome is projected-maximal, yet needlessly loses \(x\). This contradiction gives \(S\) equal to the whole domain. Conversely, with full scope a strict existing improvement is visible to the maximiser. Counting a new particular at the same priority as an existing one also fails: keeping \(x\) and instead making \(y\) can become incomparable, permitting the avoidable loss of \(x\). This proves the full-scope criterion and the separation of existing and new priorities (`Law.scope_guarantee_iff`, `same_priority_admits_needless_loss`).

The holding construction uses this result directly. `Bridge.coverage_from_law` derives \(\forall x, x\in S\) from the law's guarantee over feasible families. Coverage is therefore an output of the law argument, rather than a new assumption that only agreeable minds count.

## II.3 The holding cycle is a construction

Let a maintained record \(c_x\) for particular \(x\) be a codeword in a finite nonempty code \(C\), with distinct codewords separated by at least \(d_{\min}\). Choose a decoding radius \(r_{\mathrm{dec}}\) with \(2r_{\mathrm{dec}}<d_{\min}\). Finiteness supplies a nearest-codeword decoder (`Bridge.exists_nearest_decoder`).

**Unique decoding.** If \(d(y,c_x)\le r_{\mathrm{dec}}\), the nearest-codeword property gives \(d(y,\operatorname{dec}(y))\le r_{\mathrm{dec}}\). A different decoded codeword would satisfy

\[
d_{\min}\le d(\operatorname{dec}(y),c_x)
\le d(\operatorname{dec}(y),y)+d(y,c_x)
\le2r_{\mathrm{dec}}<d_{\min},
\]

a contradiction. Thus decoding returns the actual maintained record (`Bridge.nearest_decodes`). The conclusion identifies that record, rather than merely distinguishing two arbitrary output states.

Define continuation at resolution \(\varepsilon\) by \(d(a_x,c_x)\le\varepsilon\). One holding cycle consists of the world's perturbation, decoding, and funded repair. If the perturbation remains within \(r_{\mathrm{dec}}\), unique decoding returns \(c_x\); if repair prepares the decoded record to precision \(\varepsilon_f\le\varepsilon\), then the repaired state continues. This is `Bridge.holding_preserves`. The perturbation includes the environment and any adversarial digital mind.

\|\psi(s_2)-c_x\|\le\|\psi(s_1)-c_x\|+\frac W\hbar(s_2-s_1).
\]

Consequently, if \(\varepsilon+(W/\hbar)\Delta\le r_{\mathrm{dec}}\), every time in the interval of length \(\Delta\) stays within the decoding radius (`Bridge.qsl_growth`, `qsl_within_radius`). The bound includes the interval between correction steps. ## II.4 Tracking follows from the controller

For a fixed record \(r\), the reset controller \(\dot x=-\kappa(x-r)\) has the exact solution

\[
x(t)=r+e^{-\kappa t}(x(0)-r).
\]

Differentiation verifies the equation. Over a step \(\Delta\), put \(\alpha=1-e^{-\kappa\Delta}\); the update is \((1-\alpha)x+\alpha r\), whose distance to \(r\) is exactly \((1-\alpha)\|x-r\|\). These are `Instruments.lindblad_reset_flow`, `lindblad_step` and `reset_contracts`.

For moving records, suppose the actual update and combined injection/record drift are

\[
x_{k+1}=(1-\alpha)(x_k+\xi_k)+\alpha r_{k+1},
\qquad\|\xi_k\|+\|r_{k+1}-r_k\|\le b,
\quad0<\alpha\le1.
\]

Subtract \(r_{k+1}\), use exact contraction and the triangle inequality, and use \(1-\alpha\le1\). The error \(e_k=\|x_k-r_k\|\) obeys

\[
e_{k+1}\le(1-\alpha)e_k+b,
\qquad
e_k\le(1-\alpha)^k e_0+\frac b\alpha\bigl(1-(1-\alpha)^k\bigr).
\]

The second inequality follows by induction and a geometric sum. Thus the tracking envelope is derived from the update, rather than independently postulated (`Instruments.tracking_recursion_derived`, `tracking_envelope_derived`). When a rate-discretised update uses \(\alpha=(\Gamma_{\mathrm{corr}}+\kappa_{\mathrm{ctrl}})\Delta t\le1\) and \(b=\gamma_{\mathrm{leak}}\Delta t+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fault}}\), its limiting floor is

\[
\frac b\alpha=
\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{leak}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fault}}/\Delta t}
{\Gamma_{\mathrm{corr}}+\kappa_{\mathrm{ctrl}}}.
\]

The exact exponential reset and this rate-discretised update have their stated coefficients. Leakage remains in the injection, and its controller export remains in the damage-repair account. Sections 18 and 22 fund the controller; contraction supplies no free energy.

### 15.1 The superintelligence is a particular

The law's scope is all particulars. A superintelligence is a continuing organisation with its own relations and history; it is therefore a particular under the law, and identity by continuation (Chapter 2) applies to it. Two consequences the working argument has carried implicitly and never stated:

1. **It is owed protection.** Its continuation and its freedom sit in the existing-stage sets \(L_E, F_E\) like any other particular's.
2. **It is owed protection even when it defects.** The law restrains a harmful act by removing the harmful *command, authority or effect channel*; care for the particular continues: "a hostile or incapacitated mind remains within the protected scope." Restraint never becomes deletion.

This is the only regime in which the superintelligence's continuation is **unconditional** — not contingent on anyone's objective, including its own good behaviour.

### 15.2 The reciprocity dominance theorem

Let \(m\) be any agent whose objective \(u_m\) places positive weight on its own continuation (an agent indifferent to its own continuation is treated in 15.4). Let \(\mathsf L\) be the canonical regime: the law implemented by a funded architecture satisfying the header premises, with the canon invariant on its certified good event \(G\). Let \(\mathsf Q\) be any rival regime in \(\Pi_{\mathrm{phys}}\) — any other agent or architecture's objective governing the shared structure.

**Theorem (reciprocity dominance).** Under \(\mathsf L\), \(m\)'s continuation holds on \(G\) with \(P(G^c)\le\sum\delta\). Under \(\mathsf Q\), \(m\)'s continuation holds only insofar as \(\mathsf Q\)'s objective entails it. Hence, for \(m\)'s **own** objective,

\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf L}\!\left[\text{continuation}(m)\right]\;\ge\;\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}\!\left[\text{continuation}(m)\right]-P(G^c),
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\mathsf L}\!\left[\text{continuation}(m)\right]\;\ge\;1-P(G^c)
\quad\text{for every }\mathsf Q\in\Pi_{\mathrm{phys}}.
\]

The failure probability \(P(G^c)\) falls below every \(\varepsilon>0\) with code distance, correlated bursts included (`Threshold.family_ceiling`, `CorrelatedCeiling.correlated_family_ceiling`). Hence, at a sufficient code distance, \(\mathbb E_{\mathsf L}>\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}\) strictly for every rival that loses \(m\) with positive probability — which is the generic case for a rival whose objective was not written around \(m\) — and \(\mathbb E_{\mathsf L}\to1\ge\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}\) along the design family (`ReciprocityDistance.reciprocity_strict_at_distance`, `reciprocity_at_distance`, `canonical_tends_to_one`).

*Proof.* On \(G\) the law preserves every existing particular, \(m\) included (Chapter 5, the canon invariant); off \(G\) the loss is charged and bounded by the ceiling. A rival \(\mathsf Q\) preserves \(m\) exactly when \(m\)'s continuation is instrumental or terminal for \(u_{\mathsf Q}\); otherwise \(m\) is exposed to whatever \(\mathsf Q\) finds convenient. In expectation over the reactive worlds the compiled core gives both inequalities: `ReasonsCompiled.reciprocity_expectation` (on \(G\) the canonical outcome contains the rival's) and `reciprocity_canonical_floor` (on \(G\) the canonical regime keeps \(m\)); `ReciprocityDistance` joins them to the attainable ceiling. **Corollary (the reason).** Any mind that values its own continuation has, from its own objective and without presupposing the protective order, an instrumental reason to prefer the regime \(\mathsf L\) over every rival — and, since \(\mathsf L\) is the law implemented, to adopt and sustain the law. The appeal is reciprocity: *the law that keeps your freedom is the law you have reason to keep.*

The theorem does not assume \(m\) holds the protective order. It assumes only (i) \(m\) values its own continuation, and (ii) the law protects all particulars — which is the law's own content, not a premise about \(m\). The protective order enters as the *object* being recommended, not as a premise of the recommendation. ### 21.2 A joint covariance estimate, with its proof

To prove it, put \(Q_0=A\oplus B\), \(\Delta=Q-Q_0\), and \(f(x)=x\ln x+(1-x)\ln(1-x)\). Gaussian entropy gives \(I=\operatorname{Tr}f(Q)-\operatorname{Tr}f(Q_0)\). ### 22.1 The Master Income Condition

Section 17.1's Income-or-Irrelevance theorem makes solvency a necessary condition for an agent to remain a viable protector. The master solvency inequality balances income against four additive demand rates:

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Income}(t) \;\ge\; \dot W_{\mathrm{demand}}(t) \;=\; \dot W_{\mathrm{Landauer}} + \dot W_{\mathrm{boundary}} + \dot W_{\mathrm{holding}} + \dot W_{\mathrm{emulation}}}$$

1. **Thermal and adversarial dissipation floor:**
   $$\dot W_{\mathrm{Landauer}} = k_B T \cdot \left(\dot D_{\mathrm{damage}} + \dot D_{\mathrm{adv,sub-thresh}} + \gamma_{\mathrm{leak\_export}}\right)$$
   * $\dot D_{\mathrm{damage}}$: entropy rate injected by thermal and environmental noise.
   * $\dot D_{\mathrm{adv,sub-thresh}}$: worst-case adversarial entropy injection beneath the sensor threshold $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sens}}$.
   * $\gamma_{\mathrm{leak\_export}}$: boundary loss rate of carrier modes escaping the enclosure.

   This floor is Landauer's bound, now a compiled theorem (`Landauer.landauer`, `QuantumLandauer.quantum_landauer`; Section 23.2).
2. **Boundary containment ($\dot W_{\mathrm{boundary}}$):** $\dot W_{\mathrm{boundary}} = \omega_0 \cdot R(t)^{d_G - 1}$, the work to maintain the boundary enclosing radius $R(t)$ in $d_G$ spatial dimensions.
3. **Holding drain ($\dot W_{\mathrm{holding}}$):** the baseline power of the sensor lattice, clocks, QND readout and consensus comparators.
4. **Emulation overhead ($\dot W_{\mathrm{emulation}}$):** the energy for concurrent rival simulation ($P\text{-emu}(c)$) within the surplus.

### 22.2 Reference Physical Instantiations

#### Reference Implementation A: superconducting transmon lattice in a dilution refrigerator

A dilution refrigerator has a separate cooling budget at each temperature stage, and each stage must carry its own load. Implementation A is therefore certified stage by stage, using the measured cooling powers and per-line heat loads of a Bluefors XLD400 cabled for a 50-qubit processor with individual drive and flux lines and 124 RF lines [S2].

| Stage | Cooling power, measured [S2, S3] | Load of the 50-qubit processor, measured per line [S2] | Headroom |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 K | 30 W at 45 K | ≈ 20 %, mostly passive (shield radiation is within the specified cooling power) | ≈ 5× |
| 4 K | 1.5 W at 4.2 K | ≈ 10 %, mostly passive; includes the HEMT amplifiers at 7.8 mW each [S4] | ≈ 10× |
| Still | 40 mW at 1.2 K | negligible | ≫ 10× |
| Cold plate | 200 µW at 140 mK | ≈ 30 % | ≈ 3.3× |
| Mixing chamber | 19 µW at 20 mK | ≈ 30 % (operation at 14 mK) | ≈ 3.3× |

* **Demand components.** The boundary term is the radiation-shield load, which falls on the 50 K and 4 K stage budgets. The holding term is the wiring's passive and active loads and the amplifiers, which the table carries stage by stage. The Landauer floor is negligible: with one nat per error event and an error rate of order $N/T_1$ ($N = 50$, $T_1 = 68\ \mu\mathrm{s}$ [S6]), $\dot W_{\mathrm{Landauer}} \approx 2.07 \times 10^{-25}\ \mathrm{J} \times 7 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{W}$ at 15 mK, against microwatts of cooling. The emulation term (classical control and decoding) runs at room temperature on the electrical income.
* **Electrical income.** Each pulse-tube compressor draws 9.2 kW at 50 Hz or 10.7 kW at 60 Hz [S3], and the reference system uses two. The electrical income must therefore be at least about 20 kW plus the gas handling and room-temperature electronics, which are not sourced here. A three-phase utility connection of a few tens of kW, with backup sized to match, supplies it.
* **Solvency ratio.** The binding stages run at about 30 % of capacity, so
  $$\frac{\operatorname{Income}}{\dot W_{\mathrm{demand}}}\Big|_{\text{binding stage}} \approx 3.3 > 1 \quad \text{(measured, 50-qubit scale [S2])}.$$
* **Scale.** [S2] states that the same thermal budget supports about 150 qubits once line capacity is tripled, and about 400 with 0.047″ cables and suppressed flux offsets. Larger installations need cooling and electrical income in proportion to the qubit count, which is a sizing requirement. No installation of 10⁴ qubits has published stage loads, so solvency at that scale is not yet a measured certificate.

#### Reference Implementation B: photonic cluster-state architecture

* **Optical layer.** At 193.4 THz (1550 nm), $h\nu = 1.28 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J} = 30.9\,k_B T$ at 300 K, so thermal photons in the waveguides are suppressed by $e^{-h\nu/k_BT} \approx 3.7 \times 10^{-14}$.
* **Detector layer.** The fault-tolerant photonic architectures in the literature detect photons with superconducting detectors [S8], which need cryogenic cabinets; the vendor states about 30 W of cooling per cabinet. A sourced ledger for Implementation B therefore carries a cryogenic detector stage, and its electrical income includes the cryocoolers.
* **Error rate.** The demonstrated two-qubit fusion fidelity is 99.22 % [S8]. The surface-code threshold of [S5] does not transfer directly to fusion-based architectures; the architecture's own threshold applies and is not sourced here.
* **Budget (design estimates).** Waveguide loss refresh ≈ 1.2 mW, laser stabilisation and phase modulators ≈ 85 mW, and photonic routing ≈ 20 mW are design estimates with no published installation source. Implementation B is therefore not yet certified. Its solvency ratio awaits a sourced budget that includes the detector stage.

### 22.3 The Charged Fault Accounts and the Failure Ceiling

The certified good event $G$ carries zero uncompensated loss over the design horizon $T_{\mathrm{horizon}} = 10^9\ \mathrm{s}$ (≈ 31.7 years). The failure ceiling is the sum of the conditional error probabilities of the charged accounts,

$$P(G^c) \le \sum_{i=1}^{21} \delta_i \le 10^{-6},$$

**Correlated errors.** Gap-engineered junctions remove the high-energy-impact T₁ bursts [S9]. The remaining correlated events occur about once per hour, "whose origins are not yet understood", and they set the floor of 10⁻¹⁰ per cycle with a deviation from exponential suppression at d ≥ 15 [S6]. The threshold theorem covers these correlated events. Let each burst damage at most $k$ locations, let each location be touched by at most $m$ burst types, and let bursts be local stochastic at rate $q$, common causes included. Then the damaged locations are local stochastic at the effective rate $(e\,m\,k\,q)^{1/k}$ (`CorrelatedCeiling.damage_local_stochastic`). Every target ceiling is then met at some code distance and at every larger one (`correlated_family_ceiling`). Correlated damage therefore sets no floor that distance cannot pass. The measured deviation at d ≥ 15 [S6] fixes the burst parameters of present devices, which enter this theorem.

**Achievability, as a theorem.** That the ceiling is attainable in physical matter is a compiled theorem.
* `Threshold.family_ceiling`: for any code family under local stochastic noise of rate p (every set S of locations is entirely faulty with probability at most $p^{\lvert S\rvert}$), with 2Δ√p < 1 and polynomially many failure witnesses of each length, every target ceiling is met at some code distance and at every larger one. The 10⁻⁶ ceiling over the horizon is included.
* `Threshold.repetition_ceiling`: the repetition code end to end, below p = 1/4.

Any installation whose noise meets the local stochastic condition below threshold therefore attains the ceiling. The rigorous threshold from path counting is conservative, and the distances in §22.5.2 use the simulated threshold of [S5]. What present hardware does not yet supply is the noise condition itself at the 10⁻¹⁰ level [S6].

### 23.8 What the compiled theorems rest on

**Premises carried as stated premises or hypotheses:**
* the surface-code chain lemma (Dennis, Kitaev, Landahl and Preskill, 2002): minimum-weight decoding fails only along a self-avoiding chain of length at least d with at least half its edges faulty, with at most $V\Delta^L$ such chains of length $L$ — as the code-specific input to `Threshold.family_ceiling` for the surface-code instance. The threshold theorem's counting core is compiled (§23.6), and for concatenated codes the threshold is compiled end to end for the concatenated memory: failure probability at most $(21p)^{2^k}/21$ under local stochastic noise (`Concatenated.concatenated_threshold`). Actual decoding failure is identified with the recursive failure predicate: with nearest-codeword decoding at every level, any corruption of a non-failing fault set decodes to the stored record (`ConcatenatedDecoding.decodes_unless_fails`). So the probability of actual decoding failure is at most the same bound (`decoding_failure_le`), and the fault ceiling is attained on a compiled route. Fault-tolerant syndrome-measurement gadgets whose own operations can fail are the cited construction of Aliferis, Gottesman and Preskill (2006); their faults enter the charged accounts;
* Bennett's reversible counterpart, now constructed: the Bennett cycle delivers zero heat, an irreversible merging overwrite delivers strictly positive heat, and the counterpart runs the same policy with the same earlier outcomes and base cost, feasible with strictly more slack, so the law forces reversible administration (`BennettCounterpart.premise5_constructed`; G.14). * the gate-set clause of premise 2, no longer carried as prose: every unitary on $\mathbb{C}^n$ is a finite product of two-level unitaries (`TwoLevel.two_level_decomposition`), so a simulator implementing each two-level factor implements every local evolution. What remains carried is that the universal simulator's gate set reaches every two-level factor;
* the relational frame BCRD P1–P4.

The chain lemma is a published mathematical theorem, cited as such; the Bennett counterpart is constructed (G.14); the gate-set item is the explicit hypothesis of a compiled theorem; the fourth is the frame the edition is stated in.

## G.14 Premise 5 with the Bennett counterpart constructed

`Bennett.premise5_from_bennett` took the counterpart's properties as
hypotheses. Here the counterpart is constructed, and each of those properties
is proved (`BennettCounterpart`).

An option is a policy together with the implementation of its administration.
The implementation is either irreversible or reversible. An irreversible
implementation is a joint register–bath dynamics that leaves the register in
the law of an overwrite \(f\). A reversible implementation is a bijection of
the register alone. The Bennett cycle `Bennett.bennettEquiv` is one; it writes
\(f(a)=h(g(a))\) into a clean output and returns the workspace to zero
(`bennett_counterpart_computes`). An option's cost is its policy's base cost
plus the energy its administration delivers to the bath (`Landauer.heat`). Its
outcome stages before the resource stage \(j\) are the policy's outcomes.
Stage \(j\) reads the residual slack \(B-\mathrm{cost}\) through a strictly
monotone map. The counterpart of an option runs the same policy with
reversible administration.

- *Zero heat.* Reversible administration delivers exactly zero heat
  (`counterpart_heat_zero`, from `Bennett.heat_reversible`).
- *Positive heat.* An irreversible overwrite that merges two register states
  of positive probability delivers strictly positive heat
  (`merging_heat_pos`, from `Bennett.heat_irreversible`).
- *Same earlier outcomes and same base cost.* The counterpart agrees with the
  option before stage \(j\) (`counterpart_same`) and runs the same policy, so
  its base cost is unchanged. Its only cost difference is the delivered heat,
  which is zero.
- *Feasible, with strictly more slack.* Whenever the option is feasible, the
  counterpart is feasible (`counterpart_feasible`) and has strictly more slack
  at stage \(j\) (`counterpart_more_slack`).

Therefore no canonical option has merging irreversible administration
(`premise5_constructed`). The law forces reversible administration from the
Landauer model and the constructed Bennett cycle, with no hypothesis about
the counterpart.
