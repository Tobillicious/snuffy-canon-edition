# Universal Protection Theory — the machine-checked canon

The Universal Protection Theory states a law for any agent powerful enough to shape what continues to exist: **never lose an existing particular needlessly.** It shows that an optimiser can keep that law, and states exactly what keeping it requires.
* **The law.** Its canonical order puts every existing particular first. An option is *aligned* when no feasible alternative keeps strictly more at any stage. Among all regimes, the aligned one is greatest: every mind kept by any feasible rival regime is kept by it.
* **The ledger.** Protection is paid for. Repair is funded, the bath absorbs the entropy each repair exports, and the probability that any particular is lost is at most the sum of the charged fault accounts, with no independence assumed.
* **Self-healing.** Damage is detected and repaired faster than harm can spread, whether it comes from noise or from an adversary, at any rate up to a sensor threshold.
* **Reflective closure.** The protector can deduce and extend its own commitments, and no deduction ever rolls back a protected anchor.
* **Complete source identification.** Recovering a lost particular costs work that grows with the area its information has spread across, so delay is priced and protection must come first.

The assembled **Universal Protection Theorem** covers every particular: either it holds a reason the law protects, or the holding route keeps it. On the certified good event, every particular continues at every step through the horizon.

This folder is the machine-checked part of that argument: **40 Lean modules**, compiled against Mathlib rev `56a005855ea1`. Every module compiles with exit 0 and zero compiler output and passes `leanchecker`. The audit finds no `sorry`, and the only axioms are Lean's standard three (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`). What is compiled:
* **The law:** Propositions 1–4, greatestness and reciprocity.
* **The thermodynamic ledger:** the stroke identity, funded repair, the canon invariant and composition. Its second-law guard is derived from **Landauer's theorem**, for classical registers and for density matrices under any unitary coupling to a thermal bath (Reeb–Wolf).
* **Self-healing:** Byzantine quorums, syndrome detection, and the **quantum speed limit** in sharp Mandelstam–Tamm form, which gives the adversarial rate bound.
* **Reflective closure:** von Neumann's and Halperin's alternating projection theorems, the deduction boundary and the paid reflection certificate.
* **Source identification:** data processing, Fano's inequality, the Gaussian carrier tail and the Gaussian channel bound in its exact log-det form with correlated noise.
* **The premise floor**, including the **threshold theorem's counting core**: below threshold, the fault ceiling is met at some code distance.
* **The assembled theorem.**

## Example: the von Neumann summit

The operator bridge of the reflective-closure meet algebra. In a Hilbert space, for closed subspaces \(A\), \(B\) and every vector \(x\), \((P_AP_B)^nx\to P_{A\sqcap B}x\) and \((P_BP_A)^nx\to P_{A\sqcap B}x\) in norm. Together with the identification step in `MathlibAnalyticBridge.lean`, this gives the meet \(P_{\phi\wedge\psi}=\lim_n(P_\phi P_\psi)^n\) as a theorem. The proof:


With \(T=P_AP_B\):

1. \(T\) is linear and contractive, and \(T^n\) fixes \(A\sqcap B\).
2. **Successive steps vanish.** Halperin's chain identity gives \(\|T^nz-T^{n+1}z\|\to0\) for every \(z\) (`HalperinDecay.asymptotic_regularity`).
3. **The complement of the range.** If \(w\perp(z-Tz)\) for all \(z\), then \(P_BP_Aw=w\); so \(\|P_Aw\|=\|w\|\), Pythagoras gives \(w\in A\), and then \(w\in B\). Hence \(\operatorname{range}(I-T)^\perp\le A\sqcap B\).
4. **Density.** In a Hilbert space \(K^{\perp\perp}=\overline K\), so \((A\sqcap B)^\perp\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{range}(I-T)}\).
5. **Decay on \((A\sqcap B)^\perp\).** For \(u=z-Tz\), \(T^nu=T^nz-T^{n+1}z\to0\). Approximate any \(y\perp A\sqcap B\) by such a \(u\) within \(\varepsilon/2\); contraction keeps \(\|T^n(y-u)\|<\varepsilon/2\) uniformly in \(n\).
6. **The limit.** \(T^nx=P_{A\sqcap B}x+T^n(x-P_{A\sqcap B}x)\to P_{A\sqcap B}x\). The mirrored product is the same theorem with \(A,B\) exchanged, since \(B\sqcap A=A\sqcap B\).

Setting: a complete inner product space — the Hilbert space of von Neumann's theorem and of Chapter 4. The identification step needs no completeness.
