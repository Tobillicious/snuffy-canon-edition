### Proving Bounded Initialization: The Small-$t$ Bootstrap Surge

#### 1. Defining the Bootstrap Window ($t \le r_{\text{fence}}$)

Let the system begin at $t = 0$ with an initial micro-seed core of radius $r_0 = O(1)$ containing the minimal foundational records $I_0$.

* The time required for the wavefront to propagate across the initial core and establish the first stable shielding fence is bounded by the finite propagation speed (Lieb-Robinson limit $v_{\text{max}}$):

$$t_{\text{boot}} = \frac{r_{\text{fence}}}{v_{\text{max}}}$$


* Because $r_{\text{fence}}$ and $v_{\text{max}}$ are fixed constants of the hardware architecture, the bootstrap phase occupies a strictly finite, discrete temporal window: $t \in [1, t_{\text{boot}}]$.

#### 2. Bounding the Initialization Work ($Q_{\text{boot}}$)

During this bootstrap window, active shell routing and boundary encoding are not yet running at scale. Instead, the initial system setup incurs a fixed, one-time configuration energy:


$$Q_{\text{boot}} = \sum_{k=1}^{t_{\text{boot}}} w_{\text{initial}}(k)$$


Because $t_{\text{boot}}$ is a constant independent of the macroscopic age $t$, $Q_{\text{boot}}$ evaluates to a fixed scalar constant:


$$Q_{\text{boot}} = C_{\text{boot}} < \infty$$

#### 3. Absorbing the Bootstrap Surge into the Master Ceiling

To prove that this initialization cost does not break our asymptotic $O(t^{d_G})$ scaling for all $t \ge 1$, we examine the behavior of our total work bound across both regimes:

* **For $t \le t_{\text{boot}}$:** The work bill is bounded by the constant bootstrap cost plus small-scale routing:

$$\mathcal{Q}(t) \le C_{\text{boot}} + \alpha \sum_{k=1}^t k^{d_G - 1}$$


* **For $t > t_{\text{boot}}$:** The macroscopic asymptotic scaling $C \cdot t^{d_G}$ takes over fully.

We can formally absorb the finite initialization surge into our master constant by defining a modified universal prefactor $C'$:


$$C' = C + \frac{C_{\text{boot}}}{1^{d_G}}$$


Because $C_{\text{boot}}$ is a fixed finite number, dividing it by $t^{d_G}$ for small $t$ simply adds a bounded offset that is fully swallowed by the leading polynomial coefficient at macroscopic scales.

Thus, **there is no hidden non-adiabatic divergence at $t = 1$**. The system boots cleanly within a bounded finite-energy window, and the $O(t^{d_G})$ scaling holds universally for all $t \ge 1$. $\blacksquare$
