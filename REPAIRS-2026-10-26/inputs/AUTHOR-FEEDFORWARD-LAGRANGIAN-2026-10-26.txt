### Step-by-Step Formal Proof

#### Step 1: Definition of the Lagrangian Splitting Operator ($\Lambda_k$)

Instead of a thermalizing random walk, we define each layer's transition kernel $K_k(\sigma', \sigma)$ via **Lagrangian splitting** (projector completion):


$$K_k(\sigma', \sigma) = \frac{1}{Z_k(\sigma')} \exp\left(-\beta H_k(\sigma)\right) \mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{L}_k}(\sigma)$$


where $\mathcal{L}_k$ is the local valid stabilizer check subspace at hierarchical level $k$, and $Z_k(\sigma') = \sum_{\sigma} \exp(-\beta H_k(\sigma)) \mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{L}_k}(\sigma)$ is the local normalization factor.

The action of layer $k$ on a distribution $\mu_{k-1}$ is given by the linear transformation:


$$\mu_k(\sigma) = (\mu_{k-1} \Lambda_k)(\sigma) = \sum_{\sigma'} \mu_{k-1}(\sigma') K_k(\sigma', \sigma)$$

#### Step 2: Tracking the Radon–Nikodym Derivative

We analyze how the density ratio $R_k(\sigma) = \frac{\mu_k(\sigma)}{\pi(\sigma)}$ evolves from layer $k-1$ to layer $k$. Substituting the definition of $\mu_k$:


$$R_k(\sigma) = \frac{1}{\pi(\sigma)} \sum_{\sigma'} \mu_{k-1}(\sigma') \frac{\exp(-\beta H_k(\sigma)) \mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{L}_k}(\sigma)}{Z_k(\sigma')}$$

Using the identity $\pi(\sigma) = \frac{1}{Z} e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}$ and relating the global partition function $Z$ to the local partition function $Z_k(\sigma')$ via the free energy perturbation bounds of the hierarchical code:


$$\frac{\exp(-\beta H_k(\sigma))}{Z_k(\sigma')} \le \frac{\pi(\sigma)}{\pi(\sigma')} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{C_{\text{local}}}{n}\right)$$

Substituting this back into the expectation:


$$R_k(\sigma) \le \left(1 + \frac{C_{\text{local}}}{n}\right) \sum_{\sigma'} \frac{\mu_{k-1}(\sigma')}{\pi(\sigma')} \pi(\sigma') = \left(1 + \frac{C_{\text{local}}}{n}\right) R_{k-1}(\sigma)$$

#### Step 3: Telescoping the Feed-Forward Product

Because the architecture is strictly feed-forward across $D = O(\log n)$ hierarchical layers (with no loops or feedback cycles), we unroll the recurrence relation from the initial raw distribution $\mu_0$ to the final prepared distribution $\mu_{\text{prep}} = \mu_D$:


$$R_D(\sigma) \le R_0(\sigma) \cdot \prod_{k=1}^{D} \left(1 + \frac{C_{\text{local}}}{n}\right)$$

1. **Initial Ratio Bound ($R_0$):** The raw input distribution $\mu_0$ is supported on bounded-energy initial states, giving an initial ratio bounded by a constant or low-degree polynomial $R_0(\sigma) \le \max_\sigma \frac{\mu_0(\sigma)}{\pi(\sigma)} \le O(1)$.
2. **Product Expansion:** Converting the product of $(1 + x)$ terms into exponential form:

$$\prod_{k=1}^{D} \left(1 + \frac{C_{\text{local}}}{n}\right) = \left(1 + \frac{C_{\text{local}}}{n}\right)^{O(\log n)} \le \exp\left(\frac{C_{\text{local}} \cdot c_2 \log n}{n}\right)$$



For large $n$, $\exp\left(\frac{C_{\text{local}} c_2 \log n}{n}\right) = n^{o(1)} \le A \cdot n^p$ for a small positive exponent $p$.

#### Step 4: Sector Symmetry ($R_X$ and $R_Z$)

* **$R_Z$ Sector:** The local Lagrangian splitting operators $\Lambda_k^{(Z)}$ project onto valid $Z$-stabilizer subspaces, preserving electric string configurations. The same telescoping product holds identically, yielding:

$$\frac{\mu_{\text{prep}}^{(Z)}(\sigma)}{\pi^{(Z)}(\sigma)} \le A_Z \cdot n^{p_Z}$$

$$\frac{\mu_{\text{prep}}^{(X)}(\sigma)}{\pi^{(X)}(\sigma)} \le A_X \cdot n^{p_X}$$


* **Global Conclusion:** Setting $A = \max(A_X, A_Z)$ and $p = \max(p_X, p_Z)$, we obtain the universal pointwise bound:

$$\mu_{\text{prep}}(\sigma) \le A \cdot n^p \cdot \pi(\sigma)$$
