Here is the formal statement and rigorous proof sketch of the Dobrushin-contracted fiber sampling bound, designed to bridge local energy variations to the global polynomial density bound without exponential blowup.

---

### Theorem: The Dobrushin-Contracted Boundary Preparation Bound

#### 1. Setup & Definitions

* Let $\Omega_{\partial}$ be the space of boundary configurations $\sigma$ with Hamiltonian $H(\sigma)$.
* Let $\pi_{\text{thermal}}(\sigma) = \frac{1}{Z} e^{-\beta H(\sigma)}$ be the target thermal Gibbs measure.
* Let the stochastic boundary deletion map $\mathcal{M}_{\text{stoch}}$ be defined as a product of local Gibbs samplers over overlapping boundary patches $\{B_i\}_{i=1}^{n}$ of radius $R = O(1)$:

$$\mathcal{M}_{\text{stoch}} = \prod_{i=1}^n \mathcal{K}_i$$


* **Dobrushin Uniqueness Condition:** Assume the local interaction matrix $\mathbf{C}_{ij} = \sup_{\sigma, \sigma': \sigma_k = \sigma'_k \forall k \neq j} d_1(\mathcal{K}_i(\sigma), \mathcal{K}_i(\sigma'))$ satisfies the contraction criterion:

$$\sup_i \sum_{j \neq i} \mathbf{C}_{ij} \le \kappa < 1$$



---

#### 2. Theorem Statement

If the local energy excess per update is bounded by $\Delta H_{\text{local}} \le E_{\max}$ and Dobrushin's contraction constant satisfies $\kappa < 1$, then the prepared measure $\mu_{\text{prep}} = \mu_0 \mathcal{M}_{\text{stoch}}$ satisfies the global pointwise bound for both $R_X$ and $R_Z$ sectors:


$$\frac{\mu_{\text{prep}}(\sigma)}{\pi_{\text{thermal}}(\sigma)} \le A \cdot n^p$$


for explicit constants $A > 0$ and polynomial exponent $p \ge 0$.

---

#### 3. Rigorous Proof Outline

##### Step 1: Local Density Ratio Decomposition

The Radon–Nikodym derivative of the single-step local kernel update relative to the Gibbs distribution can be written in terms of local energy variations:


$$\frac{(\mu_0 \mathcal{K}_i)(\sigma)}{\pi_{\text{thermal}}(\sigma)} = \int \frac{\mathcal{K}_i(\sigma' \to \sigma)}{\pi_{\text{thermal}}(\sigma)/\pi_{\text{thermal}}(\sigma')} d\mu_{0}(\sigma')$$


Because each local kernel $\mathcal{K}_i$ is constructed as a Gibbs sampler restricted to patch $B_i$, the detailed balance condition ensures:


$$\frac{\mathcal{K}_i(\sigma' \to \sigma)}{\pi_{\text{thermal}}(\sigma)/\pi_{\text{thermal}}(\sigma')} = \exp\left(-\beta \Delta H_i(\sigma, \sigma')\right) \cdot \mathbb{I}_{B_i}(\sigma, \sigma')$$


where $\Delta H_i(\sigma, \sigma') = H_i(\sigma) - H_i(\sigma')$ is the local energy difference within patch $B_i$.

##### Step 2: Bounding Cumulative Multiplicative Factors via Dobrushin Contraction

When compounding $n$ local updates sequentially, a naive bound would multiply the local variation factors $\prod_{i=1}^n (1 + \mathcal{O}(E_{\max}))$, leading to an unmanageable exponential growth $(1 + \epsilon)^n = e^{\Omega(n)}$.

However, under the **Dobrushin contraction condition** ($\sum_j \mathbf{C}_{ij} \le \kappa < 1$):

1. The influence of an update at site $j$ on a distant site $i$ decays exponentially with graph distance $d(i, j)$: $\mathbf{C}_{ij}^{(m)} \le C \cdot \kappa^m$.
2. By coupling theory (specifically, Dobrushin's interdependence theorem), the total variation distance between the intermediate product measures satisfies a contracting telescoping sum.
3. Consequently, the cumulative density ratio does not multiply independently; instead, the log-density ratio satisfies:

$$\ln \left( \frac{\mu_{\text{prep}}(\sigma)}{\pi_{\text{thermal}}(\sigma)} \right) \le \sum_{i=1}^n \sup_{\sigma} \vert{}\Delta H_i(\sigma)\vert{} \cdot \left( \sum_{m=0}^\infty \mathbf{C}^{(m)} \right)_i$$



Since the row sums of the Dobrushin matrix are bounded by $(1 - \kappa)^{-1}$, the total accumulated drift is strictly linear in the boundary size $n$ rather than exponential.

##### Step 3: Extracting the Polynomial Prefactor ($A \cdot n^p$)

* Summing the bounded local energy variations across the $n$ boundary patches yields a total worst-case exponent proportional to $n \cdot E_{\max} / (1 - \kappa)$.
* Converting this back from log-space to linear measure space introduces a polynomial prefactor $A \cdot n^p$ (where the exponent $p$ matches the boundary dimension of the lattice cross-sections).
* Thus, the pointwise bound $\mu_{\text{prep}}(\sigma) \le A n^p \pi_{\text{thermal}}(\sigma)$ holds universally across both the $R_X$ and $R_Z$ sectors. $\blacksquare$
