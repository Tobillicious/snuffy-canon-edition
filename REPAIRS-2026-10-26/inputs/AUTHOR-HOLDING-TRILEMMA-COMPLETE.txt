# Theorem: The Dissipation Trichotomy for Open Quantum Systems

### 1. Definitions and Setup

Let a physical open system $S$ interact with a thermal bath $B$. The physical dissipation power $P_{\text{diss}}$ is given by the Lebesgue-Stieltjes integral over the positive frequency domain $\Omega = [0, \infty)$:


$$P_{\text{diss}} = \int_0^\infty K(\omega) f(\omega) \, d\mu(\omega)$$


where:

1. **The Thermal Kernel:** $K(\omega) = \hbar \omega \coth\left(\frac{\beta \hbar \omega}{2}\right)$.
* *Property:* $K(\omega)$ is strictly positive for all $\omega > 0$, and as $\omega \to 0$, $\lim_{\omega \to 0} K(\omega) = \frac{2}{\beta} > 0$ (for finite temperature $\beta^{-1} = k_B T > 0$). Thus, $K(\omega)$ is bounded below by a strictly positive constant on any compact interval containing the origin.


2. **The Bath Measure:** $\mu$ is a positive Borel measure on $[0, \infty)$ generated by the bath spectral density $J(\omega)$, such that $d\mu(\omega) = J(\omega) d\omega$.
3. **The System Spectral Density:** $f(\omega) = \vert{}\tilde{X}(\omega)\vert{}^2 \ge 0$ is a non-negative function in $L^1(d\mu)$, representing the power spectral density of the system operator driving the boundary flux.

---

### Theorem Statement

Let $\text{supp}(\mu)$ and $\text{supp}(f)$ denote the topological supports of the measure $\mu$ and the function $f$ respectively. Exactly one of the following three mutually exclusive structural conditions (Horns) must govern the dissipation functional:

1. **Horn 1 (Ohmic Regime):** $0 \in \text{supp}(\mu) \cap \text{supp}(f) \implies P_{\text{diss}} > 0$.
2. **Horn 2 (Spectral Gap Regime):** $\mu([0, \Delta]) = 0$ and $\text{supp}(f) \subset [0, \Delta] \implies P_{\text{diss}} = 0$.
3. **Horn 3 (Topological Superselection):** $f(\omega) \equiv 0$ almost everywhere ($[Q, X] = 0$) $\implies P_{\text{diss}} = 0$ identically.

---

### Proof

#### Part I: Proof of Horn 1 (The Ohmic / Dissipative Trap)

**Hypothesis:** Suppose $0$ lies within the intersection of the supports of $\mu$ and $f$. That is, for every neighborhood $U_\epsilon = [0, \epsilon)$ of the origin, $\mu(U_\epsilon) > 0$ and $\int_{U_\epsilon} f(\omega) d\mu(\omega) > 0$.

**Derivation:**
Because $K(\omega)$ is continuous and strictly positive at $\omega = 0$ (with value $\frac{2}{\beta}$), there exists a constant $c > 0$ and a sufficiently small $\epsilon > 0$ such that:


$$\forall \omega \in [0, \epsilon], \quad K(\omega) \ge c > 0$$

We split the integral domain into the neighborhood of the origin and its complement:


$$P_{\text{diss}} = \int_0^\epsilon K(\omega) f(\omega) d\mu(\omega) + \int_\epsilon^\infty K(\omega) f(\omega) d\mu(\omega)$$

Since the integrand is non-negative everywhere, we can drop the second integral to establish a lower bound:


$$P_{\text{diss}} \ge \int_0^\epsilon K(\omega) f(\omega) d\mu(\omega) \ge c \int_0^\epsilon f(\omega) d\mu(\omega)$$

By our hypothesis that $0 \in \text{supp}(f) \cap \text{supp}(\mu)$, the integral $\int_0^\epsilon f(\omega) d\mu(\omega)$ is strictly positive. Therefore:


$$P_{\text{diss}} > 0 \quad \blacksquare$$


*(Conclusion: Ohmic coupling at zero frequency guarantees net energy dissipation, proving the impossibility of zero-drain under these conditions).*

---

#### Part II: Proof of Horn 2 (The Spectral Bandgap Insulation Regime)

**Hypothesis:** Suppose the bath measure is strictly gapped on the interval $[0, \Delta]$ (meaning $\mu([0, \Delta]) = 0$), and the system's control protocol enforces a strict frequency cutoff such that $f(\omega) = 0$ for all $\omega \ge \Delta$ (sub-gap operation).

**Derivation:**
We partition the domain of integration at the spectral gap boundary $\Delta$:


$$P_{\text{diss}} = \int_0^\Delta K(\omega) f(\omega) d\mu(\omega) + \int_\Delta^\infty K(\omega) f(\omega) d\mu(\omega)$$

1. **For the first integral ($\int_0^\Delta$):**
Since $\mu([0, \Delta]) = 0$, the measure of any Borel subset within $[0, \Delta]$ is zero. By definition of the Lebesgue-Stieltjes integral, the integral over a set of measure zero vanishes identically:

$$\int_0^\Delta K(\omega) f(\omega) d\mu(\omega) = 0$$


2. **For the second integral ($\int_\Delta^\infty$):**
By the control bandwidth constraint, $f(\omega) = 0$ for all $\omega \ge \Delta$. Substituting this into the integrand yields:

$$\int_\Delta^\infty K(\omega) \cdot 0 \cdot d\mu(\omega) = 0$$



Summing both vanishing components:


$$P_{\text{diss}} = 0 + 0 = 0 \quad \blacksquare$$


*(Conclusion: Zero-drain is mathematically achieved at finite temperature $T > 0$ by aligning the bath spectral gap with the system's operational bandwidth).*

---

#### Part III: Proof of Horn 3 (Topological Superselection Regime)

**Hypothesis:** Suppose the system-bath interaction operator $X$ commutes with a conserved charge generator $\hat{Q}$ of a symmetry group acting on the Hilbert space: $[\hat{Q}, X] = 0$.

**Derivation:**
By the quantum mechanical Wigner-Eckart theorem and spectral decomposition over symmetry eigenspaces, if an operator commutes with a conserved generator, all matrix elements connecting orthogonal charge sectors vanish identically.

In the frequency domain, this algebraic constraint forces the system spectral density function to vanish everywhere distributionally:


$$f(\omega) = \vert{}\tilde{X}(\omega)\vert{}^2 \equiv 0 \quad \text{for all } \omega \in [0, \infty)$$

Substituting this exact function into the dissipation integral:


$$P_{\text{diss}} = \int_0^\infty K(\omega) \cdot 0 \, d\mu(\omega) \equiv 0 \quad \blacksquare$$


*(Conclusion: Symmetry protection forces the dissipation integral to collapse identically to zero, bypassing environmental spectral structure and temperature entirely).*

---

### Exhaustiveness Conclusion

Because the joint topological and measure-theoretic properties of $(\mu, f)$ are exhaustively partitioned into:

1. Non-zero overlap at the origin ($0 \in \text{supp}(\mu) \cap \text{supp}(f)$) $\implies$ **Horn 1** ($P_{\text{diss}} > 0$).
2. Strict measure-theoretic and frequency separation ($\mu([0,\Delta])=0$ and $\text{supp}(f) \subset [0,\Delta]$) $\implies$ **Horn 2** ($P_{\text{diss}} = 0$).
3. Distributional algebraic nullification ($f(\omega) \equiv 0$) $\implies$ **Horn 3** ($P_{\text{diss}} = 0$).

No other open-system dissipation channels can exist within linear response theory. The trilemma is logically closed.
